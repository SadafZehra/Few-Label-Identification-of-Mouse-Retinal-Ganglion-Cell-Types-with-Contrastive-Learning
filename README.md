# Few-Label Identification of Mouse Retinal Ganglion Cell Types with Contrastive Learning
## 1. Background and question

The mouse retina has more than 40 functional types of ganglion-cell-layer (GCL) cells. They are usually identified from two-photon Ca²⁺ responses to a full-field **chirp** and a **moving bar** (Baden et al., 2016). The All-GCL dataset (Gonschorek et al., 2026) provides responses of more than 80,000 GCL cells recorded under standardised conditions, with functional group labels assigned by a classifier.

Labelling cell types by hand is slow and expensive, and recordings from different sessions differ (batch effects). TRACE (NeurIPS 2025) showed that contrastive learning gives useful representations of neural time series.

**Question.** If we learn a representation of cell responses **without labels** (contrastive, SimCLR-style), how well can we identify cell types when only **1 %, 5 % or 10 %** of the cells are labelled? And does the representation carry less session-specific (batch) information than the raw traces?

## 2. Method

1. **Data.** Only the compact summary table `all_GCL_table.parquet` (82,173 cells, 214 columns, about 8.4 GB) is downloaded from Hugging Face (`eulerlab/all-gcl`, no login). The full NWB files (about 32 GB) are not used.
2. **Column detection.** The notebook picks the columns for chirp trace (`chirp_snippets`, 259 time points), moving-bar trace (`bar_snippets_t0`, 24 time points), functional group (`cluster_id`), super-group, quality indices (`chirp_qidx`, `bar_qidx`), classifier confidence (`prob_cluster`) and session (`experimenter`). They can be overridden in the `COLUMNS` config.
3. **Quality control.** Keep cells with a good chirp **or** good bar response (chirp QI > 0.35 or bar QI > 0.6, as in Baden et al., 2016), classifier confidence ≥ 0.5, and groups with at least 50 cells.
4. **Session-level split.** Test cells come from recording sessions that were never seen in training.
5. **Contrastive encoder.** A two-branch 1D-CNN (chirp branch + moving-bar branch, mixed into a 64-dimensional embedding) trained SimCLR-style on all training cells **without labels**, with time-series augmentations (amplitude scale and offset, small time shift, smoothing, noise, masking). 60 epochs.
6. **Label-efficiency experiment** at 1 %, 5 %, 10 % and 100 % of training labels (3 random seeds each, stratified sampling), evaluated on held-out sessions. Compared representations and classifiers:
   - raw traces, PCA (40 components, fitted on training sessions only) and the contrastive embedding, each with **kNN** (cosine) and **logistic regression**
   - a **supervised CNN** (same architecture) trained only on the labelled cells
7. **Levels.** Fine level = functional groups; coarse level = super-groups (OFF, ON-OFF, Fast ON, Slow ON, ...).
8. **Batch-effect test.** How well can the *session* be predicted from each representation with a 5-fold kNN? Lower means less batch information.
9. **Figures** generated by the notebook: label-efficiency curves, t-SNE maps by type and by session, per-type F1, session predictability, example responses.

## 4. Results (balanced accuracy on held-out sessions, functional groups, mean of 3 seeds)

| Method | 1 % labels | 5 % labels | 10 % labels | 100 % labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw traces + kNN | 0.116 | 0.136 | 0.160 | 0.195 |
| raw traces + logistic | 0.114 | 0.147 | 0.151 | 0.221 |
| PCA (40) + kNN | 0.118 | 0.149 | 0.171 | 0.208 |
| PCA (40) + logistic | 0.089 | 0.153 | 0.195 | **0.281** |
| contrastive + kNN | 0.108 | 0.134 | 0.154 | 0.158 |
| contrastive + logistic | **0.127** | 0.184 | 0.218 | 0.266 |
| supervised CNN (labelled only) | 0.106 | **0.200** | **0.230** | 0.273 |

Chance level: 0.014.

**Batch effects** (session predictability from held-out-session cells, lower = less batch information):

| Representation | Session balanced accuracy | Chance |
|---|---|---|
| raw traces | 0.628 | 0.500 |
| PCA (40) | 0.663 | 0.500 |
| contrastive | 0.626 | 0.500 |

## 5. Interpretation

- All methods are far above chance (0.014) but absolute accuracy is low (below 0.30 even with 100 % of the labels). This is a hard 69-class problem with cross-session generalisation, and the labels themselves are classifier outputs.
- **With a logistic-regression head, the contrastive embedding is the best method at 1 % labels (0.127) and clearly better than PCA and raw traces at 5 % and 10 %** (for example 0.184 vs. 0.153 and 0.147 at 5 %). So unlabelled data does help when few labels are available.
- **The contrastive embedding with kNN is not better than PCA or raw traces** (0.134 vs. 0.149 at 5 %), so the benefit depends on the classifier. The embedding is more useful for a linear head than for distance-based retrieval.
- A **supervised CNN trained only on the labelled cells** matches or beats the contrastive model at 5 % and 10 % labels (0.200 and 0.230 vs. 0.184 and 0.218). In this pilot, contrastive pre-training helps mainly at the very lowest label budget (1 %) and does not beat a supervised network from 5 % upward.
- With all labels, PCA + logistic (0.281) is as good as or slightly better than everything else.
- **The contrastive embedding does not reduce batch information in a meaningful way** (0.626 vs. 0.628 for raw traces); the differences are tiny.

## 6. Limitations

- **Labels are classifier outputs**, not manual annotation. "Accuracy" means agreement with the All-GCL classifier (trained on Baden et al. 2016). High-confidence filtering reduces but does not remove this.
- **Few sessions.** The automatically chosen session column was `experimenter`, giving only 8 groups with 2 held out. The test set (2,080 cells) therefore comes from 2 groups, and the batch-effect test is a 2-class problem (chance 0.5). Results may depend strongly on which sessions were held out; a proper session or retina identifier and several held-out splits would be more reliable.
- Mean accuracy is given without confidence intervals; standard deviations over the 3 seeds are saved in `label_efficiency_summary.csv`.
- Trial-averaged responses only. TRACE builds positive pairs from different subsets of single trials, which needs the NWB files.
- Augmentations were chosen by hand; large time shifts can change the meaning of a cell type (response latency is informative).
- 69 groups were kept (the dataset description mentions G1 to G46), so check which label column (`cluster_id`) was used.
